About Award Insights

A free, public look at where federal research money goes to U.S. colleges and universities, alongside the peers you choose. It describes the data and applies the same rules to every institution; it doesn't rate institutions. Corrections and questions: info@awardinsights.org.

The five tools and their sources

Federal Award Insights

USAspending.gov Award Data Archive, FY2022–26, 31 agencies (files dated November 6, 2026; data through September 30, 2026). Project grants, cooperative agreements and contracts, counted as obligations by fiscal year. Subawards come from USAspending's subaward records, as reported by prime recipients.

NIH Award Insights

NIH's own award records (RePORTER), FY2022–26 (FY2025–26 pulled November 14, 2026; earlier years October 26). Each award counts at its total cost in the fiscal year NIH records it. Hospitals are left out unless a university claims them in HERD, and researchers aren't named.

NSF Award Insights

NSF's own award records, FY2022–26 (pulled October 31, 2026). Each award counts at its full intended value in the year it was made, with an option to see what NSF has obligated so far.

Research Activity Designations

Carnegie Classifications 2025 Research Activity Designations, with R&D spending from NSF's HERD survey (FY2020–25) and research doctorates from IPEDS (AY2020–21 to 2024–25).

Research Portfolio Trends

NSF's Higher Education R&D (HERD) survey, FY2016–25: the R&D spending each institution reports, by funding source, federal agency and field, with personnel, money passed through, medical school and clinical trials, the kind of research and type of cost, as each institution classifies them.

Why figures differ between sources

  • An institution's own figures usually follow its own fiscal year, may count spending or full award values, and include subawards, so they can differ from these without either being wrong.
  • Federal and NIH Award Insights both cover NIH money, from USAspending and from NIH's own records; totals are close, but one institution or year can differ more.
  • Federal and NSF Award Insights: NSF Award Insights counts each award once, at full value, in the year it was made; Federal counts what was obligated each year.
  • Awards and R&D spending: Portfolio and Designations use spending reported to HERD from all sources; an award is spent over several years, so the two don't move together.
  • Hospitals count with a university only where HERD would; independent hospitals are left out.
  • Timing: each source updates on its own schedule, and recent years are still being revised.

How Federal Award Insights counts money

  • Obligations, not award values. Dollars are what agencies obligated in each fiscal year, including continuing payments on earlier awards.
  • What counts. Project grants, cooperative agreements and contracts: the kinds of awards institutions generally apply for, though USAspending doesn't record whether a grant was competed. Formula and block grants, direct payments, loans and student aid don't count, nor do formula programs recorded as project grants that have been identified (such as HBCU Title III).
  • New award codes. Since late 2025, a grant under USAspending's new codes counts when its program was mostly project grants and cooperative agreements in FY2022–25. ARPA-H's research awards count whatever their code.
  • Projects, not operations or buildings. Grants for running an institution (such as a university-owned airport or campus buses) don't count. Construction grants are listed under programs but kept out of totals, ranks and peer comparisons, as HERD leaves construction out.
  • Pullbacks. Money pulled back from an award comes off the year it was given, so no year goes below zero. Most pullbacks are routine revisions and closeouts, not cancellations.
  • Earmarks. Congressionally directed spending is listed and tagged but kept out of totals, ranks and peer comparisons.
  • Aligned with HERD. Each institution includes the affiliates it would report to NSF's HERD survey, such as research foundations and university-affiliated labs (Johns Hopkins includes its Applied Physics Laboratory). Contracts to run national laboratories and other federally funded R&D centers are left out, as are hospitals run as separate organizations (about $17.4B over FY2022–26).
  • Recipients. Awards are matched by recipient ID: the IDs each college lists in IPEDS plus its affiliates' IDs, each added with a recorded reason. System offices, independent research institutes and foreign institutions are their own entities; for-profit institutions are left out.
  • Comparing administrations. The By agency trend and the Analysis tab compare months since January 20, 2025 with the same months of FY2022–24, so seasons cancel out. FY2022–24 includes one-time money (the Inflation Reduction Act, the infrastructure law, pandemic programs), and a change can reflect budgets, timing or policy; the site shows what changed, not why.

Every counting rule, tool by tool

The full notes behind each tool, folded. Open one to read it.

Federal Award Insights
  • Sources: USAspending.gov Award Data Archive, FY2022–26 assistance and contract transactions for 31 agencies (files dated November 6, 2026), joined to recipients by UEI using the IDs colleges list in IPEDS plus affiliates HERD would count with them; IPEDS 2025; 2025 Carnegie Classifications; NSF HERD.
  • Dollars are the sum of each award's federal actions in a fiscal year, so they include continuing increments on awards made before FY2022 and net out de-obligations.
  • No year below zero: money pulled back comes off the year it was given. Returns of money obligated before FY2022 (about $3.3B nationally, mostly closeouts of older awards) are not subtracted, since that money was never counted, so totals run slightly above a straight USAspending sum. The Awards view shows the actual net figure for any award this affects and doesn't list awards whose only activity was returning older money.
  • Out of scope: formula and block grants (including USDA capacity funds), direct payments, loans and student aid: Education's Title IV programs and HRSA's school-held student loan funds (Health Professions Student Loans, Loans for Disadvantaged Students, Nursing Student Loans and the Nurse Faculty Loan Program, about $215M over FY2022–26).
  • Formula streams reported as project grants are excluded: Strengthening HBCUs Part B, HBCU FUTURE Act, Historically Black Graduate Institutions, Tribal Colleges Part F, Predominantly Black Institutions formula grants and HEERF.
  • New award codes: F001 (grant) and F002 (cooperative agreement), used from late 2025, don't separate project from formula grants; programs with no earlier record wait for review.
  • Operations and buildings: grants for running an institution (university-owned airports, campus transit, school buses, housing block grants, weatherization; about $244M nationally) are left out. Grants for buildings and infrastructure (such as NIH construction grants and EDA public works; about $404M) are listed under Programs as facilities and construction.
  • Earmarks are identified by Assistance Listing, award-number series (such as FIPSE's P116Z) or award description, and kept out of peer program lists too.
  • Contracts exclude management-and-operating money for federally funded R&D centers (JPL, Lincoln Laboratory, SEI and the DOE national laboratories). University-affiliated research centers and university labs count with their university. "Competed" follows each contract's reported extent of competition.
  • Hospitals count with a university where HERD would: hospitals the university owns, and hospitals whose research it reports to HERD as its own (such as Cincinnati Children's with the University of Cincinnati). If a university's HERD report includes research at a hospital that files awards under its own recipient ID and isn't matched here, the award tools show less than HERD.
  • Recipients: public and private nonprofit IPEDS institutions, plus system offices, independent research institutes that report to HERD (such as Scripps Research and Woods Hole) and foreign institutions as their own entities. Affiliated research foundations and units count with the campus HERD reports them under; registrants naming a system, board or central office count with the system office. An institution showing $0 may hold awards under an ID not yet matched.
  • Timing: NSF often obligates a multi-year grant in its first year, while NIH usually obligates year by year, so year-to-year changes for one agency partly reflect timing.
  • FY2026 has ended but is still being revised: agencies report some actions late, and Defense publishes contracts about 90 days after the fact, so its recent months are thin.
  • Current annual funding is the obligations recorded in the 52 weeks ending on the latest action date; an award netting below zero over those weeks counts as zero.
  • Scheduled to end uses each award's period-of-performance end date as of its most recent action, which agencies extend for no-cost extensions and renewals, so it shows exposure rather than expected losses.
  • Latest counts new awards (grants coded as new, and contracts or orders with no modification) by the date they were made, in rolling windows ending on the latest action date, compared with the same weeks a year earlier; dollars are the amount obligated when each award was made. The latest eight weeks are still filling in.
  • Comparing administrations: months from February 2025 (the change falls mid-month) are compared with the same months of FY2022–24, the previous administration's full fiscal years.
  • Programs moved between agencies: money counts under the agency that awarded it. Where an agency has newly awarded another agency's programs (for example, Education programs awarded through Labor in FY2026), the agency pages say so.
  • Subawards are as reported by prime recipients. Duplicate filings count once, and no prime award's subawards count above the award's own value. Money that reaches an institution through a state under formula or block programs is left out. FY2026 is still being reported.
  • HHS is split into its operating divisions. NIH is broken out by the institute or center that made each award (the Office of the Director covers the Common Fund, research infrastructure programs including the primate centers, and All of Us). CDC, HRSA, SAMHSA and CMS each award through a single grants office, so they are broken out by program, with their contracts in one row. "HHS: other divisions" holds ACF, ACL, FDA, ASPR, AHRQ, IHS and the Office of the Secretary. HERD reports all of these as HHS.
  • Programs are Assistance Listings (CFDA); NSF reports by directorate here, and its program-level detail is in NSF Award Insights.
  • Peers and ranks: peer groups, R&D bands and ranks cover the institutions in the Carnegie universe. Ranks are competition ranks among the institution and its peers (T- marks a tie); none is shown where the institution has nothing in that row or the peer group has fewer than 4 members.
NIH Award Insights
  • Counting: a multi-project grant counts once, at its parent's total. Federal Award Insights counts NIH obligations by date from USAspending, which is why the two differ slightly.
  • Hospitals are left out unless the university claims them in HERD (such as Cincinnati Children's with the University of Cincinnati).
  • New, renewed and continuing come from NIH's application type: new competing awards, competing renewals, non-competing continuations of multi-year awards, and supplements.
  • Programs: pairing an institute with an activity code means a peer comparison shows mechanisms at specific institutes. A program counts as held with any counted award in FY2022–26.
  • Departments are as NIH records them.
  • The site shows institutions, institutes, award types and departments, never researchers' names.
NSF Award Insights
  • Recipients: joined to institutions with the same crosswalk as Federal Award Insights. NSF's awards to non-university recipients (research consortia, federal labs, nonprofits, companies) and to private for-profit institutions are left out; the tool's notes give the share of NSF dollars covered.
  • Dollars: continuing grants are paid in annual increments, which is why full intended value keeps years comparable. "Obligated to date" files payments under the year an award was made. Federal Award Insights counts NSF obligations by year from USAspending, so its NSF figures differ.
  • Programs: in the awards list, an award under several programs shows at its full value.
  • Divisions: NSF renamed many of its divisions as sections in fall 2026 and recoded about half of these awards, so a division name here can differ from the award's page on nsf.gov.
  • Peers come from the full Carnegie universe, whether or not they hold NSF awards. Ranks are among the institution and its peers (T- marks a tie), shown with at least 4 peers. Institutional factors: 2025 Carnegie Classifications, IPEDS, NSF HERD.
  • An institution showing $0 may hold small awards not yet matched.
Research Portfolio Trends
  • Source: HERD public microdata, joined to IPEDS by UNITID plus a crosswalk for reporters with no or outdated IPEDS IDs. Private for-profit institutions are left out.
  • Short form: it asks only for totals by funding source and broad field.
  • Reporting changes (†): the figures are shown as reported.
  • Fields and funding: four detailed fields were first reported separately in FY2016; foreign funding is broken down by source from FY2016.
  • Peers: the same peer settings as the award tools, drawn from the institutions on this page. Independent research institutions and system offices are outside the Carnegie classification, so they have no peer group. Peer medians are among peers with a figure for that item.
Research Activity Designations
  • Spending is total R&D expenditure from HERD microdata, joined to IPEDS by UNITID plus a crosswalk for reporters with missing or outdated IDs.
  • Research doctorates are IPEDS Completions "Doctor's degree – research/scholarship," first major. AY2020–21 to 2022–23 reproduce Carnegie's counts for every designated institution exactly.
  • Flags: institutions outside the 2% match are flagged for combined campus reports, NSF revisions, or units that don't reconcile.
  • 2028 window: FY2024 and FY2025 spending are already reported.
  • Thresholds: R1 at least $50M in R&D and 70 research doctorates; R2 at least $5M and 20; Research College or University at least $2.5M.

Peers

Peers are the institutions that share the characteristics you choose to hold constant, such as research designation, R&D expenditure, control and degree focus, from the 2025 Carnegie Classifications and IPEDS. Comparisons with peers use the median.

How it's made

Award Insights is built with the help of AI tools, but its rules and judgment calls are human: what counts, how each institution’s affiliates are matched, and how unusual cases are handled. Every figure is computed by scripts from public data, and each update is reviewed before it’s published.

Corrections and questions

If a figure looks wrong, or a recipient seems matched to the wrong institution, write to info@awardinsights.org. It helps to name the institution, the page and the figure.

Independence

Award Insights is independent. It has no formal affiliation with any college or university, federal agency or other organization, is not endorsed by any of them and does not speak for them. Its figures are its own compilation of public data, not official statements or figures of any institution or agency; for those, go to the institution or agency itself. Figures are provided for information and can contain errors; corrections are welcome.
